## The bug found on the way (affects the existing `Scan`)

**Writing a bone rotation and then calling `mixer.update(0)` on a paused action leaves that action
unable to write the pose again.** Reproduced minimally: reset `bone.quaternion`, `mixer.update(0)`,
then seek again → the bone reads untouched, forever. `scanSeam` borrows the playback mixer, so a
**second `Scan` press in one session measures a stale pose and reports the clip clean**. Measured:
first `sampleClipPoses` −21.1°, every later call identity.

Fix used here: `sampleClipPoses` runs a **private `AnimationMixer`**, and reports bind values for
bones the clip does not animate (rather than resetting the scene, which is what poisons the mixer).
Regression test asserts a repeated scan reproduces the first result. **`scanSeam` still has the
defect** — left alone as out of scope.

## Task C6, runtime half — the jaw pivot in the shipped GLB

`apply improvement`: the blend was fixed but `public/models/wyvern-v2-rig.glb` still rendered the old
pivot, so the improvement was invisible. An export would have reverted C5/D2–D4, so the same change was
applied to the GLB directly by `tools/blender/recenter_v2_jaw_pivot.mjs`.

**What "move the pivot" means in glTF.** There is no rest pose to edit — a joint's world position is
`W_parent · T_channel · A_channel`, and all 18 clips key `Jaw` translation, rotation *and* scale. So the
pivot lives in `node.TRS` **and** in every translation sample. The change:

    T_channel += δ_local                              (δ_local = T_new − T_old, parent frame)
    A_channel  = (R_new · R_old⁻¹) ⊗ A_channel        (axis corrected, motion kept)
    IBM_jaw    = inverse(W_target) · (W_old · IBM_old)

`R_new` is the blend's construction — local `X` exactly world-lateral, `Y` the current bone direction
projected into the sagittal plane, `Z = X×Y` — so the two halves agree. Blend→glTF is the plain Y-up map
`(x,y,z) → (x,z,−y)`: fitted over the 91 joints C6 did not move, scale `1.00000009`, worst residual
`0.0014`. The jaw was the worst of all 92 at **`83.99`**, which independently confirmed the GLB still
carried the old pivot.

**Result.** Pivot `[3.623, 867.769, 583.727] → [0, 785, 565]` (84.938 units). Asset `b5f1c1ef…` →
**`04dbda5c…`**, `6,732,156 → 6,738,716 B` (+6,560 = one 92×`MAT4` IBM; the channels were rewritten in
place). Exactly three signature fields changed — `bindMatrices.sha256`, `nodes[Jaw].translation`,

## Jaw amplitude restore — the open question above, closed

**Cause.** The three diverging clips are exactly the ones a D2/D3/D4 gain pass touched, and `breath` /
`attackAir2` / `idle` are exactly the ones it never did. Those passes amplify every rotation channel about
its own bind (`bind · qpow(bind⁻¹q, g)`), and `Jaw` is in neither `PROTECTED` nor `PANEL_COUPLED`. The mouth
was amplified with the rig.

**The export relation, measured not fitted.** `channel(k) = rest_glb · basis(0)⁻¹ · basis(k)` reproduces the
three never-gained clips to `0.0000° / 0.0000° / 0.0001°`; `rest·basis(k)` is off by the constant
`basis(0)` (the C6 compensation of `2.090°`). The controls are the instrument.

**`death` was different, and that is the finding.** Restoring the jaw is free in `attack` and `attack2` (the
jaw never holds a frame's maximum rate). In `death` it set the maximum in **158 of 240 frames**, so the
restore alone takes the clip from `p95 888 / mean 354` to `p95 560 / mean 305` — under floors of
`734.2 / 351.8`. The shipped `death` passed its mean gate by **0.6%**, and that 0.6% *was* the impossible
pose. Four repairs measured and rejected: uniform gain (unsafe — 21 bones already past 150°, `qpow` wraps
past 180° and turns 179° into −38°; it scores *best* and is still wrong), uniform gain ≤1.05× (accel binds
at 1.05 while p95 needs 1.31), tail ripple (invisible — the metric is a per-frame max and the tail never
holds it), neck-only 1.6× (clears at 3.9%, which is fitting), gain+smoothing (safe on accel, takes the peak
1738 → 1383 for nothing).

**Kept:** D2's own map with a per-bone cap — amplify only while `excursion × gain ≤ 165°`, hold a bone
already past it. `gain 1.9` amplifies 29 of 39 non-coupled channels (spine, neck, head, tail 1–10, wing
roots/wrists, leg roots/feet/toes) and holds 10. `peak 1728` (was 1738), `p95 862`, `mean 418` (V1 70% →
83%), `accel 44,935` (was 44,919), worst margin **17.4%** against the shipped 0.6%.

**The membrane set had to move as one.** A per-bone cap broke the panel gate at `34.65°` against a `1°`
limit — panels with small excursions were amplified while their digits were held. The 52 coupled channels
now share one gain from their worst member (179.29°), which is `1`, so the whole membrane is untouched.

**`readGlb` returns a view.** `binary: bytes.subarray(...)` aliases the input, so in-place writes mutated
the trial source and the first probe compounded every gain into the next (`death` read `p95 6973`). Trials
now start from `Buffer.from(sourceBytes)`.

**Recovery trick worth remembering:** the pre-restore asset is reproducible — `git show HEAD:…` is
`b5f1c1ef…`, and `recenter_v2_jaw_pivot.mjs --write` on it returns `04dbda5c…` byte-for-byte (which also
re-confirmed that pass independently).

## Session 4 (14:20–15:10) — `attack`'s 0.5% margin investigated; NOT fixed, and now well understood

**Question:** can `attack`'s mean margin (1664.3 against a 1673.7 ceiling = 139.3% of V1) be improved?

**Answer: not by any gain reshape. Four approaches measured, the fourth rejected for a reason the gate
cannot see.** Evidence: `assets/rig-audits/2026-10-22-c6-jaw-pivot/attack-punctuation.json`, probe
`tools/blender/probe_attack_punctuation.mjs` (no write path), doc 41 note 20.

**First correction to my own earlier claim:** V1 `AttackBite` is NOT "one violent bite over a quiet 6.5 s".
Measured, it is continuously violent — 0.25 s bins run at mean 1100–2600 deg/s for the whole 0–4.75 s,
with 66 of 388 frames at ≥0.5× peak. Only the last ~1.2 s is quiet. V2's accent *fraction* (19/89 = 21%)
is actually slightly higher than V1's (17%). The real difference is that V2 is time-compressed: it does
in 1.5 s what V1 does in 6.5 s, so every frame is busy.

**The mean budget (decisive measurement):**
| bone | frames holding the frame max | share of the mean |
|---|---|---|
| Head | 39 / 89 | 51.1% |
| Jaw | 19 / 89 | 26.1% |
| wing panels (5 bones) | 15 / 89 | 19.8% |
| tail (2 bones) | 17 / 89 | 3.1% |

**`Jaw` is parented to `Head`** and holds a constant local rotation (13.55°) outside the t=0.10–0.40
mouth opening, so the jaw's *world* rate equals the head's for most of the clip → Head carries ~77%.

**Four approaches, measured:**
1. Uniform gain on everything → best worst-margin ~5.5%; `0.85× → p95 3732` under the 3799.8 floor. Just
   moves the thin margin from the mean channel to the p95 channel.
2. Uniform gain on `PANEL_COUPLED` alone → **near no-op**, mean 1664 → 1637 even at gain 0. The panels
   that hold the max are static *at bind* (excursion 0.0); a gain about bind does nothing to them and
   their world motion is inherited from ancestors further up the chain.
3. Uniform gain on head/neck → kills the peak: 4931 → 3471 at 0.70×, under the 3830 floor.
4. **Time-varying gain (full on the strike, `bg` outside) → REACHES THE TARGET**: mean 1422, worst margin
   0.5% → 15.0%, peak and p95 held exactly, accel unchanged, 0 excursions grew. **Rejected anyway.**

**Why (4) is rejected — and why the gate is blind to it.** The wing is a 6-deep chain
(`Shoulder > Elbow > Wrist > Hand > Digit > Panel`). A chain's composed angular velocity picks up a term
proportional to the sum of the per-bone gain derivatives, so a gain that varies in time injects a jerk
wherever it changes. Measured with the window on the peak frame: the rate at t=0.317 rises
**2023 → 4670 deg/s (2.3×)** on `L_WingPanel3_1`. Moving the window edges into the clip's genuinely quiet
regions (centre 0.465, half 0.365) leaves 11 frames still rising, largest +861 deg/s (+42%) at t=0.250 on
`L_WingPanel0_0`. **`measureClipDynamics` takes the per-frame MAXIMUM across bones, so a rate rise reads
as MORE energy and only improves the gate numbers** — an instrument that rewards the artifact.

**What did pass in variant (4)** (so the next attempt doesn't re-check them): peak/p95 held exactly, accel
unchanged at the quiet-edges variant, zero channels ended further from bind than they started, and the Jaw
exclusion works — `restore_jaw_amplitude.mjs` reads only the Jaw channel (never the Head), so attenuating
the head does not make it re-fire.

**Verdict: `attack` needs authored motion in the blend** — a sharper, more punctuated bite with a quieter
anticipation and recovery. The 0.5% margin is a pass and is recorded as an open item rather than traded
for a membrane jerk. Asset untouched throughout (`f5d768a2…`, verified by hash).

**Tooling note:** the Write tool silently dropped a `]` from a one-line Map construction, producing a
file that `node --check` rejected with a confusing "Unexpected token ')'". Caught by bracket-depth
counting, fixed with a node string replace. Same class as the Read-staleness trap already in memory.
