# The Dynamics Gate (Stage 0)

**Date**: 2026-10-21
**Status**: Instrument landed. V2 fails it. Stage 3 not started.

---

## 1. Why a new channel was needed

Every gate in this repo before now measured **smoothness**:

| Gate | What it measures |
|---|---|
| `tools/audit-all-clips.mjs` | loop seam residual, root travel, bank mirror residual |
| `tools/audit-wyvern-rig.mjs` | ground contact, footprint, detector self-tests |
| `test/wyvern-v2-kinetics.test.js` | hold thresholds, feed wiring |
| `scripts/validate-wyvern-clips.mjs` | clip presence, naming, contract |

A clip that holds still is **perfectly smooth**. It passes every one of them. That is
the hole: there was no measurement of how hard a clip actually moves, so fourteen
stages of iteration were free to optimise toward stillness without anything
objecting.

This document records the channel that measures it, the V1 reference derived from
it, and what V2 currently scores.

## 2. Definition

Measured by `scripts/wyvern-dynamics.mjs`, reported by `tools/audit-dynamics.mjs`:

1. Sample every bone's **world** quaternion at 60 Hz through an `AnimationMixer`.
2. Per frame, take the **maximum over bones** of `|dq| / dt`.
3. Report `peak`, `p95`, `mean` in deg/s, plus guard channels.

World space, not local: a local-only measure misses inherited parent motion, which
is most of what a wingbeat actually is.

Only **deforming** bones (skin weight > 0) are sampled. This changes no number —
measured across all 13 V1 clips the largest difference is 0.9 deg/s (`Roar`) —
because a zero-weight `_end` tip mirrors its parent's world rotation exactly and
therefore reports an identical angular step. It changes only *which bone is named*:
`Bone020_end_0269` becomes `Bone020_0132`. That is the point. A report that blames
an unkeyable export tip cannot be acted on.

### Guard channels

`peak` alone is trivially gameable — one keyframe spike satisfies it while the clip
still reads as sludge. So:

| Channel | Rule | Catches |
|---|---|---|
| `sustained` | peak frame must have a neighbour ≥ 0.8 × peak | a 1-frame pop with no shoulder |
| `accelMax` | `\|dw\|` per frame, deg/s² | a 2-frame pop that `peak` cannot hide |
| `accents` | frames at ≥ 0.5 × peak | **reported, not gated** — see §7 |

## 5. What the numbers actually say

An earlier draft of this work claimed V2's problem was "flatness rather than
amplitude" — that V2 held a *higher* mean while reaching *half* the peak. The
measurements do not support that, and the claim is retracted here rather than
quietly dropped. Crest factor (peak ÷ mean) moves in **both** directions.

Three distinct defects, not one:

**1. Amplitude — broad.** The median mapped clip carries 41% of its reference
peak. Cruise is representative: 0.48× peak, 0.56× p95, 0.52× mean. V2 moves
less, roughly uniformly.

**2. Transients — narrow and severe.** `attack` and `takeoff` collapse to 8–9%
of reference peak while their means hold at 16–29%. Crest factor falls to 0.57×
and 0.27×. These are precisely the two clips whose V1 cousins are the most peaky
(crest 4.58 and 7.12), so they are where a uniform amplitude reduction is most
visible — and they are the two clips a player feels most.

**3. The opposite failure — `idle`.** V2's idle is not gently reduced, it is
**frozen between rare twitches**. `p95` falls to 7.7% of reference — the worst
ratio in the table by a factor of three — while crest factor *rises* to 2.33×.
A peak-amplitude threshold alone would score this clip as merely quiet and miss
that it is dead for most of its duration. This is why `p95` is gated separately
from `peak`.

`attack2` is the only clip exceeding its reference peak (1.145×) and is
simultaneously 2.55× spikier than reference — wrong *shape*, not insufficient
motion. Fixing it by adding more amplitude would make it worse.

## 6. Loop length is not the cause

V2 ships nine clips at exactly **6.0000 s with exactly 145 keys** — `cruise`,
`bankLeft`, `bankRight`, `dive`, `glide`, `hover`, `idle`, `rest`, `scout`. That
uniformity looks like a generator emitting fixed-length loops, and an earlier
draft of this document proposed that this caps the dynamics a clip can express.

**That hypothesis is rejected.** Counting wingbeat cycles directly from the
per-frame angular-velocity series (excursions above 50% of peak, halved):

| Clip | Duration | Half-cycles | Implied frequency |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| V1 `FlightCruise` | 6.00 s | 12 | 1.00 Hz |
| V2 `cruise` | 6.00 s | 13 | 1.08 Hz |

Pennycuick's wingbeat law for this wyvern (15.7 m span, ~45 m² wing area,
~800 kg) predicts ≈0.7 Hz, so both sit inside the plausible band. A 6-second loop
holds the right number of beats. See `docs/2026-10-21-research-notes.md` for the
derivation.

What differs is amplitude, on the same beat count. Re-timing the clips in Stage 3
would not fix them; restoring amplitude — especially the transients lost by
`attack` and `takeoff` — is the lever.

> **Correction, 2026-10-22.** The table above is wrong for `cruise`, and the
> method is why. "Half-cycles above 50% of peak" counts a *secondary* bump as a
> beat, and it is not stable under a change of peak: a gain raises the peak, the
> bump stops crossing the threshold, and the count moves on a clip whose timing
> never changed. Measured with the estimator `scripts/validate-wyvern-clips.mjs`
> actually uses — the dominant DFT bin of the wingtip elevation in the Body frame,
> which is indifferent to secondary bumps — V2's whole flight group beats at
> **0.500 Hz** against V1's **1.000 Hz**. That is the half of the deficit this
> section attributes to amplitude, and retiming `cruise` by `0.5` closes it with no
> pose change at all: `0.958×` peak, `1.034×` mean, `maxMembraneX` at `t = 1.0 s`
> rising to `2952.7` because the sampled frame now lands on the extension. The
> banks needed both — the retime and a `1.95×` gain for the remaining stroke
> depth. See doc 41, Task D4, and `assets/rig-audits/2026-10-22-d4-flight-group/`.


The fixed 6-second length remains a *stylistic* uniformity (nine clips the same
duration regardless of what they depict) and V1 is not uniform:

| | V1 | V2 |
|---|---|---|
| FlightCruise | 6.0000 s, 28 keys | 6.0000 s, 145 keys |
| AttackBite / attack | 6.4667 s, 321 keys | 1.5000 s, 37 keys |
| Takeoff | 6.5000 s, 359 keys | 3.0000 s, 73 keys |
| Death | 10.3000 s, 548 keys | 4.0000 s, 97 keys |
| Idle | 4.0333 s, 136 keys | 6.0000 s, 145 keys |

V1's attack is 4.3× longer than V2's and carries 8.7× the keys. That is where the
transients live — but the evidence says the cause is the *content* of those
seconds, not the loop length itself.

## 7. Traps recorded

Four things cost real time here. They are written down so the next attempt does
not repeat them.

**CUBICSPLINE tracks are 12 floats per key, not 4.** V1's tracks use glTF
`CUBICSPLINE`, so `track.values` holds three entries per key (in-tangent, value,
out-tangent). Reading `values[i * 4]` yields **tangents**, which are unnormalized
and frequently `[0, 0, 0, 0]`. It looks exactly like a file full of corrupt
keyframes and it is not — every real value at `values[i * 12 + 4]` has magnitude
1.0000. A whole afternoon went into diagnosing "corruption" that did not exist.
The measurer never touches `track.values`; it samples through an `AnimationMixer`,
which is correct per-track and is the only safe way in.

**Typed-array buffers are shared between tracks.** V1 has 0 shared track objects
but **429 shared `values` buffers** — including across clips (`Bone039_021.scale`
is shared by AttackBite and AttackClaw). Mutating `track.values` in place to
"repair" a track silently corrupts its neighbours, which then look corrupt. Any
future repair pass must deep-copy first.

**`accents` is not a gate.** It was in the first version of `GATE` and had to be
removed: V1 `takeoff` scores 2 accents against V2's 93 for the same clip. The
channel is too sensitive to re-timing to discriminate quality. It survives in the
report because it is the clearest single signal of a flat clip, but gating on it
would fail clips that are merely paced differently.

**`bankLeft` and `bankRight` produce near-identical maxima** (691.73 both, to 4
dp) which looks like a duplicated clip. It is not. Max-over-bones is
mirror-invariant, so a mirrored pair *must* agree on `peak`, `p95`, and `mean`;
their peak joints differ (`R_WingD2P2` vs `L_WingD2P2`) and their clip hashes
differ (`4d6dbdb8…` vs `1f543bb9…`). Verified, not a defect.

## 8. Running it

```bash
npm run assets:audit-dynamics       # V1 table
npm run assets:audit-dynamics:v2    # gate V2 against the V1 reference
```

Direct use:

```bash
node tools/audit-dynamics.mjs <glb>                          # measure
node tools/audit-dynamics.mjs <glb> --write-reference out    # emit reference
node tools/audit-dynamics.mjs <glb> --baseline ref --strict  # gate, exit 1 on fail
```

Tests: `test/wyvern-dynamics-reference.test.js` (5) proves the reference is
generated; `test/wyvern-v2-dynamics.test.js` (6) proves the gate can fail. Both
were confirmed live by mutation — normalising `rate` by clip duration turns the
re-timing test and the reference test red.

## 9. Stage 3 exit criteria

Stage 3 is done when `npm run assets:audit-dynamics:v2` passes, which requires:

- every mapped clip at ≥ 70% of reference `peak` and ≥ 80% of reference `p95`;
- `mean` within 0.7–1.4× of reference (guards against buying peak with jitter);
- `accelMax` ≤ 1.25× reference (guards against buying peak with a pop);
- `sustained` true everywhere;
- and `idle`'s crest factor back under 1.5× reference, so the fix is not just
  added spikes over a frozen body.

`test/wyvern-v2-dynamics.test.js` asserts V2 currently fails. **When Stage 3
lands, invert that assertion** — the file says so inline.

classes a gain cannot fix — see doc 37 §6 for what each one actually needs.
